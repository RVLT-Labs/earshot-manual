Limits and known behaviour

Read this before you trust Earshot on a show.

Earshot is work in progress. It runs and is tested on one Apple Silicon Mac. This page says what has been measured, what has been tried end to end, what has never been tried, and where the known gaps are. Nothing here is softened.

The short version
  • Speech recognition makes mistakes. Treat a phrase automation as a convenience beside your normal cue calling, not instead of it.
  • It has not been tried with a real microphone on real comms, a real console, a phone, Windows or Linux desktop windows, two computers on a real network, or in a real venue.
  • Cloud speech has never been run against the real Deepgram service.
  • Safe stops what Earshot would send next. It cannot recall what has already left the computer.

Not in this build yet​

These are planned. They are either not built, or built but not usable from the app. Do not rely on any of them.

FeatureWhat you will see
"Play a sound" and "Show or hide the window" actionsYou can add them, but they do not run. The automation shows a problem saying so. The other actions in the same automation still run.
A signed installerUnsigned downloads are published for macOS Apple Silicon, Windows x64 and Linux x86_64. The Mac app is signed ad hoc only, not with an Apple Developer ID, and is not notarised. The headless host passed a demo startup smoke test on each system. Windows and Linux installer interfaces and desktop windows have not been tested. There is no Intel Mac installer.
Turning on https for Earshot's own addressEarshot serves plain http. There is no setting to give it a certificate.
Encrypted links between Earshot computersThe link screen offers "Encrypted (https)", but it has not been proven end to end in this build, and it needs a certificate this computer trusts. Treat links as not encrypted.
Choosing the network interface or web portThere is no Setup screen for them. The headless program (earshotd) takes --bind and --port. See earshotd.
Importing a speech model from a folder (for a venue with no internet)There is no screen for it. Download the model once while you have internet. See Models.

Speed​

Measured on one Apple M3 Pro (12 logical CPUs), with the release build, nothing else running. Test recordings played on every channel at the same time, continuously, for 60 seconds. That is far denser than real intercom traffic.

"First words" is measured from when the speaker starts. "Final line" is measured from when the speaker stops.

  • At 16 channels all 224 final lines were transcribed, none lost.
  • About 1.5 to 1.6 GB of memory was in use.
  • One Spotlight indexing job was using about one core at the time.

The goals were first words under 0.7 s and a final line under 1.5 s.

  • Typical times meet them at 4 and 8 channels.
  • At 16 channels the typical first words (0.74 s) just miss.
  • The slowest 5% miss the first-words goal everywhere, and miss the final-line goal at 8 and 16 channels.
  • Other computers have not been measured. Intel Macs, Windows and Linux machines may be slower.

What Behind means, and what to do about it, is in Reading the transcript and Troubleshooting.

What has been tried, and what has not​

One full run of the cue path​

The finished host was run start to finish on one Mac with the real models and recorded speech (the fixture recordings) played into four channels, the show Armed, and a local program listening for the OSC packets.

  • 12 of 12 spoken lines were transcribed, each final line 0.83 to 1.39 seconds after the speaker stopped.
  • "Lighting cue 42, go." sent one OSC packet to the lighting address with the number 42. "Sound cue 7, go." sent one to the QLab address with 7. The two "Standby …" lines sent nothing. Exactly two packets went out.
  • The glossary changed "click ladder" to "click louder" in the stored text and kept what was heard. "Q42" became "cue 42".
  • Clips were saved on the two channels that had them switched on, and the replay address returned a sound file. A line on a channel with clips off had none.
  • 14 translations, none failed: German to English, English to Spanish, and German to Spanish by way of English.

Voices and secrets, on the real host​

With the real models and three looping recorded channels:

  • Two voices were learned through the real screen (about 36 seconds each, 16 to 17 seconds of speech). No voiceprint went to the browser.
  • At Balanced, each channel carrying one voice named every line correctly. On a channel mixing five lines from three voices, the two learned voices were named correctly (13 named lines, none wrong) and the third, never learned, got no name in the first run.
  • An admin's view of the settings showed a stored key as "••••". Saving settings with the marker kept the real key on disk and stored the marker nowhere.
  • A bug was found and fixed during this check: in Lanes, the time and name floated beside the text and squeezed it to a sliver. They now sit on their own row.
  • A people-limited automation was tried as described in Automations limited to people.

Linking, on the real host​

Two hosts were run on one Mac and linked through the real screen. See Showing another Earshot computer's channels.

Not tried​

  • A real microphone.
  • Comms-quality audio. All the recorded voices are clean, computer-made speech. The voice settings were tuned on them.
  • A real console, and MIDI, web requests and TCP OSC against real gear. They have been tried only in automated tests against stand-ins on one Mac.
  • Phones and tablets. The join page, install, and small screens have not been tried on real devices.
  • Cloud speech with a real key.
  • Windows and Linux.
  • Two separate computers on a real network, including finding each other (Bonjour) between machines, and a link code on a host that requires one.
  • https, for linking.
  • The desktop app running (its tray, pop-outs and the default port) has not been tried by hand.
About the pictures in this manual

The screenshots in this manual come from the web app's built-in mock show, or from a demo host playing a scripted transcript. They show the real screens with made-up lines. None of them is a real show.

Accuracy​

  • Accuracy in a loud venue is unverified. It has been tried only on synthetic voices and a built-in microphone. The recogniser still makes mistakes on clean recordings: it dropped "du" from "Kannst du bitte…" and "I" from "I need the click…". Treat every cue as something to confirm.
  • The first word can be clipped when an input opens mid-sentence. Earshot keeps 0.3 seconds of audio from before speech starts. That covers live inputs that were already open. It does not cover the first words after an input is (re)connected while someone is already talking.
  • Languages. Parakeet covers English and 24 other European languages. Whisper small is experimental and has never been run.
  • Voice recognition has only been measured on computer-made voices. Several of them share a speech engine and so sound more alike than most people do. Real people on a real noisy party line may do better or worse. The default, Balanced, was kept on the condition that this manual says so plainly, and it may need changing after the first recording of a real party line. See People.

Translation​

  • A reading aid, never a cue call. Automations never see a translation.
  • Show jargon is often mistranslated. In the test, "Sound cue 7, go." came out in Spanish as "Suena a señal 7, vamos." and "kick drum" as "tambor de patada".
  • German to English and English to Spanish (and German to Spanish by way of English) have been run. The other pairs in the Models list are untested downloads, and their descriptions start with "Untested."
  • A viewer can pick a language that few lines can reach. The lines that cannot be translated stay in the original, and the host counts them as failed.
  • Translation models were built and run on the Apple Silicon Mac only.

See Read in another language.

Cloud speech​

Cloud speech has never been run against the real Deepgram service. It has been tested against a stand-in server only, and some details of how Deepgram answers are unverified. Before you rely on it, try one channel with a real key.

Whether the model-improvement opt-out that Earshot sends by default changes Deepgram's price is also unconfirmed.

See Cloud & audio.

Hardware and systems​

  • Tested: one Apple Silicon Mac, the built-in microphone, and played-back recordings (the simulator).
  • Windows and Linux builds are untested. The desktop app has only been built, unsigned, on macOS. The network socket settings that make Safe discard unsent OSC data are proven on macOS only. Stopping a shell command's child programs when you press Safe is untested on Windows.
  • Dante, MADI and other many-input hardware is untested, as is unplugging a USB interface mid-show.
  • The tray (menu bar) menu, its Armed tick box, pop-out windows and Keep on top have not been tried by hand. The tray's Armed tick arms with one click, without the hold the window requires. Do not use it as your normal way to arm.
  • Replay was checked against the real host, but nobody has yet confirmed that it is audible on a phone or tablet speaker.
  • Earshot is a program on a computer. If the computer sleeps, Earshot stops listening.

Known gaps in the show-control path​

  • Safe cannot recall what has already left the computer. See Safe and Armed.
  • The Armed-from-the-start rule has small edges. Earshot notices the start of speech about 30 milliseconds late, so a cue word that begins that close before you press Arm can still count as armed. The other way round, right after a channel's audio starts, is paused, changes device or has a gap, a cue that begins within about a third of a second after you press Arm can be treated as "heard before the show was armed". Arm first, then speak.
  • Run now and web hooks are tied to Armed or Safe when the engine receives them, a moment (normally under a thousandth of a second) after the request arrives. A Run now pressed as a test in Safe at the very instant someone else arms could really send.
  • Pressing Safe twice. In a very rare tie between two Arm or Safe commands arriving at the same instant, a Safe can be answered with "conflict". The screen then shows the show's real state; press Safe again.
  • A line whose beginning Earshot did not catch (it was restarting that channel) never fires. Activity says "the start of this line was missed".
  • A MIDI send that waits too long is dropped ("not sent: the MIDI port was busy for more than 1 s"), so a MIDI cue is on time or it does not go.
  • Someone on the network who can forge sender addresses can get a real console's trigger messages rate-limited. They cannot stop its Safe.
  • A firing that Earshot refuses (for example because of the runaway limits) still uses up that automation's cooldown.
  • A MIDI feedback loop is limited only to about ten firings a second, until you make the show Safe. See MIDI.
  • The glossary changes finished lines only. Draft text on screen is as heard.
  • Cloud speech does not record, per line, which engine produced it.
  • A linked channel does not say when its last line arrived, and a computer can call itself anything (so the address is shown beside the name). The first link after this computer restarts has no gap notice.
  • Names can be missing while CAM++ is loading, the voice recognition queue is full, or the audio is too short or unclear. Transcription falling behind does not switch off background voice naming.

Security you should know about​

  • Earshot starts in Open mode. Anyone on the network can view and operate the show, including arming it. Change this before you put Earshot on a network you do not control. See Who can connect.
  • Everything travels as plain http. Passwords, link codes and transcripts are readable by anyone who can listen on the network.
  • OSC has no sign-in. OSC input is off by default. Read OSC input before you turn it on. Arming by OSC works only over TCP.
  • Shell-command actions are off unless an admin turns them on. See Consoles and controllers.

Automatic discovery and overlapping voices in v0.5.0​

Automatic discovery and consecutive-turn detection have been tested with synthetic voices only. Similar voices, noise and short replies can be misidentified or left unnamed. Detected profiles can be renamed without another reading passage; controlled learning remains available.

Two-speaker overlap separation is experimental and off by default. The development M3 Pro took about 8.6 seconds to separate four seconds of synthetic audio; it is not yet real time. Quiet speech can be omitted, and isolated outputs may contain artifacts or incorrect words. More than two simultaneous speakers are not supported. Normal transcription continues while the model prepares or is occupied.

This release provides a macOS Apple Silicon package only. Windows and Linux builds are on hold.
