Privacy

Earshot writes down what people say on comms. This page says what is kept, where, who can read it, and what leaves the computer.

The short version. Everything is kept on the host computer, in one folder. Speech is recognised on that computer. If you never choose a cloud option, no audio and no text goes to any company.

What is stored, and where​

Earshot keeps everything on the host computer, in one folder:

SystemFolder
macOS~/Library/Application Support/Earshot
Windows and LinuxThe operating system's data folder, in a folder called Earshot. (Not tested on those systems.)
AnyThe folder you name with the environment variable EARSHOT_DATA_DIR (or --data-dir for earshotd).
In that folderWhat it holds
earshot.dbThe transcripts of every session, markers, and personal link records. For a line changed by the glossary or by number tidying, it holds both the changed text and what was heard.
show.earshot.json (and a .bak)The show: channels, groups, automations, glossary, people, settings. The password is stored as a hash. Cloud keys and the link codes of linked channels are stored here too. People's learned voices are stored here as well. None of these is ever sent to a browser, and none is in an export.
models/The downloaded speech and translation models.
clips/Saved audio, for channels with Keep audio for replay on.

More about each file, and how to back the folder up, is in Data and files.

How the folder is protected​

  • The folder is private to your user account. A folder Earshot creates can be opened only by you, and the show file, its backup and the database are readable only by you. (On macOS and Linux. A folder that already existed keeps the permissions it had; the files inside are still made private.)
  • Logs never contain what was said, or any secret. Lines sent for translation are logged by length only.
  • Earshot does not use a proxy for speech, translation or automation web requests, even if this computer has one set in its environment. Only model downloads use it.

Audio​

  • Audio is not saved unless you ask. Earshot turns audio into text and does not keep the audio. The exception is a channel with Keep audio for replay switched on. It saves the audio behind each spoken line on the host, in clips/. Typed lines have none. Anyone who can read that channel can replay it. See Replay a line's audio.
  • Saved audio is limited. Under Setup, Cloud & audio, Keep for (days) and At most (MB) set the limits (0 means no limit). Clips older than the limit are deleted. If the total is over the size limit, the oldest go first. The limits are checked when Earshot starts, every 10 minutes, when you change them, and as clips are added.
  • Clearing a transcript makes its audio unreachable at once. The files are deleted as well. A clip that finishes being written after its line was cleared is deleted and never announced.
  • Live audio never goes to a phone, tablet or another computer. Only text does. The one thing a browser can fetch is the saved clip behind a single line, on a channel where you switched that on.

Who can read what​

  • Who can read the transcript. Anyone who can connect. In Open mode that is everyone on the network. Roles control who can do things, but any role can read. See Who can connect and Roles.
  • Who can see what was heard. The original text of a changed line goes to Operators and Admins only. In an Open show, and with the shared password, everyone is an Operator, so everyone sees it. Only personal-link Viewers do not. (On a channel with "Filter profanity" on, the original is never shown, because it would show the words the filter hid.)
  • Learned voices identify people. They stay on the host, are never sent to any browser, and are never in an export. Names on lines go to every role. See People.
  • What others see of your settings. Nobody, including an admin, is sent a saved cloud key, link code or password. Viewers and operators also get web header values, webhook paths and shell command text hidden, and web addresses cut back to the host name. Exports are hidden the same way. See Saved secrets.
warning

Earshot starts in Open mode, and serves plain http. On a network you do not control, anyone on it can read the transcript, and anyone listening on it can read what travels. Choose Password or Personal links before the show. See Who can connect.

What goes over the network​

WhatWhen
The transcript, to the browsers of the people connectedWhenever someone has Earshot open. It goes to devices on your network that connected to the host.
Speech and translation model downloads (GitHub; Hugging Face for Whisper small and the translation models)When you press Download.
The messages your automations send (OSC, MIDI, web requests, shell commands)While the show is Armed.
Bonjour announcementsIf Announce on the network (Bonjour) is on. It is on by default.
Lines from a linked channel, and its link codeBetween the two Earshot computers, while a link is on. With Not encrypted (http) both travel readable on the network. https links have not been proven.
Audio from a channel set to "Deepgram (cloud)", to DeepgramOnly for channels you set to cloud speech and confirmed, and only if a key is saved. Words from your glossary travel with it, in the request address. Drafts stay on this computer. Every request also carries Deepgram's model-improvement opt-out.
The text of each translated line, to DeepL or Anthropic (Claude)Only if you chose that translator under Cloud & audio and confirmed. Audio is not sent.

Nothing else leaves the computer. If you never choose a cloud option, no audio and no text goes to any company.

Things worth knowing about the cloud options​

  • Cloud speech is per channel. There is no switch that sends every channel out. Each one asks you to confirm. See Cloud & audio.
  • While cloud speech is paused after repeated failures, Earshot sends one short piece of audio about every 30 seconds to test the service. A little audio therefore still leaves while it says Paused.
  • Your glossary travels with cloud speech. On a Deepgram channel, the words in your glossary (which may be crew names) are sent to Deepgram as hints.
  • Cloud speech has never been run against the real Deepgram service. See Limits and known behaviour.
  • A cloud translator sees the text of every line it translates, for every viewer reading in another language.

Clearing​

There is no button for deleting past sessions in this build.

To start a fresh history:

  1. Stop Earshot.
  2. In the data folder, delete earshot.db and the files beside it whose names start earshot.db.
  3. Delete the clips folder.
  4. Start Earshot again.

This also removes the personal links. Everyone who joined with a personal link or an invite will need a new one.

Deleting those files does not touch the show (show.earshot.json): your channels, automations, glossary, people and settings stay.

To remove lines without stopping Earshot, a transcript can be cleared: by an automation with the action Clear the transcript, or by a console at the Full control level. Clearing deletes the lines of the current session, for one channel or for every channel, and their saved audio with them. Past sessions are not touched.

Telling the crew​

Everyone on a channel Earshot listens to is being written down, and on a channel with Keep audio for replay on, recorded. Depending on where you work, the law or a union agreement may require you to tell people, or to get their agreement, before you do that. Earshot does not tell them for you.
