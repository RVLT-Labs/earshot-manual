Patch inputs and make channels

A channel in Earshot is one stream of comms: a party line, a talkback, a radio. The Patch screen turns the inputs of an audio interface into channels, many at a time. This is the fastest way to set up a show.

Who can do this

Admins only. Operators and viewers see "Setup needs admin access".

Before you start​

  • Plug the audio interface in and check the operating system can see it.
  • Know which input carries which comms channel. Earshot numbers inputs from 1, the same as the interface.
  • Download the speech models first, or the channels you make will have nothing to transcribe with. See Speech models.

Make channels from inputs​

  1. Open Setup, then Patch.
  2. Under Audio device, choose the interface. Earshot opens each device once, however many inputs you use.
  3. Tick the inputs to listen to. Each tick becomes one channel. Tick this page ticks a whole page of inputs at once, and Clear ticks unticks them.
  4. Under Name channels, type a prefix if you want one. The input number is added, for example "USB 1".
  5. Press Create. The button says how many: "Create 4 channels".

The mock host's Patch screen, with a made-up two-input device.

Under the device, Earshot shows what it found: the number of inputs, the sample rate and the audio system, and the instruction "Tick the inputs you want as channels. Inputs that already have a channel are ticked and named; untick one to delete its channel."

A patched input shows its channel's name and a live level meter. Speak on that line and watch the meter move. If it does not, the input number is wrong, the device is not delivering audio, or the operating system has not given Earshot permission to use the microphone.

Worked example: a four-channel interface​

You have a four-input USB interface. Inputs 1 to 3 carry the stage manager's party line, the audio party line and the lighting party line. Input 4 is not connected.

  1. Setup, Patch. Choose the interface under Audio device.
  2. Tick inputs 1, 2 and 3. Leave 4.
  3. In Name channels, type Comms.
  4. Press Create 3 channels.

You now have three channels called "Comms 1", "Comms 2" and "Comms 3". Open Setup, Channels, and rename them "Stage Manager", "Audio" and "Lighting", and give each a colour. The Feed shows the new names at once, on every device.

Interfaces with many inputs​

Inputs are shown 32 to a page. Next inputs and Previous inputs move between pages. Tick this page only ticks the page you are looking at, so work a page at a time.

Many-input hardware is untested

Dante, MADI and other many-input hardware has not been tried with Earshot. Neither has unplugging a USB interface in the middle of a show. Try your own hardware, at full channel count, before you rely on it.

If the device is not in the list​

  • Press Rescan devices after plugging it in.
  • If the screen says "No audio input devices were found. Plug one in, then rescan. You can also add simulator or text-only channels under Channels.", the operating system is not offering any input to Earshot. Check the system's sound settings and microphone permission.

Remove a channel from Patch​

Untick a patched input. Earshot asks first ("Delete name?") and spells out what goes: "name will be removed from the show, with its settings. This cannot be undone."

The same box lists the groups the channel is in ("It is removed from that group.") and any automations that use it ("It will still point at a channel that no longer exists."). Fix those automations afterwards, or they will not hear what you expect.

Then tune each channel​

Patch makes channels with sensible defaults. Open Setup, Channels, and pick a channel to change:

  • its name, colour and short name;
  • its language, or leave it on "Detect automatically";
  • whether to show words as they are spoken (drafts);
  • Gain and the High-pass filter (100 Hz) for a quiet or rumbly input;
  • whether people can type into it, and whether to keep audio for replay.

Every setting is explained in Channels.

Channels that are not audio inputs​

Patch is only for audio inputs. Two other kinds of channel are made under Setup, Channels, Add:

Source typeWhat it is for
Simulator (WAV file)Plays a recording as if it were live. Good for rehearsing automations without the crew.
Typed text onlyNo audio. People type lines into it. Useful as a notes channel.

A third kind comes from another Earshot computer: see Showing another Earshot computer's channels.

Check your work​

  • Every channel in the list shows a meter that moves when someone speaks, and no No input, Offline or Error badge. Status and badges explains each one.
  • A test line on each channel appears in the Feed under the right name.
