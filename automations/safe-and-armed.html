Safe and Armed

Every automation in Earshot sits behind one switch. In Safe, nothing is sent to a console, a controller or a web address. In Armed, automations that match really send. This page is the one to read twice.

Speech recognition makes mistakes

Treat a phrase automation as a convenience that sits beside your normal cue calling, not instead of it. Keep Safe as the default until you have tried every phrase on the Test bench and said it out loud on the real comms.

The two states​

The Armed or Safe control is in the header on every screen.

StateWhat it means
SafeAutomations that match are logged as "would have fired" (marked Dry run in Activity). Nothing is sent.
ARMED (red, with a red band across the header and "ARMED" in the browser tab title)Automations that match really send OSC, MIDI and web requests, and run shell commands if you have allowed them.

The control while the show is Safe. Mock show, not a real show.

The same control while the show is Armed. Mock show, not a real show.

The Automations screen repeats the state in a line at the top. In Safe it reads "Safe. Automations that match are logged as “would have fired”, and nothing is sent." In Armed it reads "ARMED. Automations that match will really send OSC, MIDI and HTTP."

Earshot starts Safe. The one exception is Start armed (Setup, Show control), which is off by default. See Start armed below.

Arming​

Who can arm

Operators and admins see the control. Everyone else sees a display-only "Safe" or "ARMED". Remember that in Open access mode everyone on the network is an operator. See who can connect.

There are two ways to arm, and both are deliberate:

  1. Hold "Hold to arm" for about 0.7 seconds. Its tooltip says "Hold for 0.7 seconds to arm".
  2. Or press and release it once, and then press Arm now in the box that opens, "Arm automations?".

Pressing "Hold to arm" once opens this box. Mock show, not a real show.

Once the show is Armed, the header turns red and stays red on every screen, for everyone.

The whole screen while Armed. Mock show, not a real show.

Going back to Safe​

  • Press Make safe. (The same button shows "Safe" while the show is Safe.)
  • Or press Alt+Shift+S from anywhere on the screen. This shortcut can make the show Safe. It can never arm.
  • A console can also make the show Safe by OSC, from any device, as long as OSC input is on. See OSC input.

Make sure everyone who may need to press Make safe knows where it is before the show starts.

If the screen has lost the host​

If the screen loses its connection to the host, the control shows "Safe — connection lost, state unknown" (or Armed). Arming is not possible from that screen, and the arm button's tooltip says "Connection lost: arming is not possible".

Press Make safe anyway. It also goes by a second route, so it can get through when the live connection cannot.

If Earshot cannot confirm Safe​

A red notice says "Could not confirm Safe — the host may still be armed." with a Retry button. Keep pressing until it clears. Do not assume the show is Safe until the header says so.

Safe always wins​

Arm and Safe are applied in the order the host receives them, and the last one wins. On top of that, Safe is given every advantage:

  • A Safe press cancels a hold in progress, a confirm box that is open, and an Arm that is still on its way.
  • A Safe press blocks arming for one second.
  • An Arm that has not gone through within 2 seconds is dropped, so a Safe never waits behind it.
  • If someone else's Arm reaches the host after your Safe, the screen says "The host is armed. Your Safe was overtaken — press Make safe again."

The same order covers the app, the API and OSC together. A Safe from any of them beats an Arm from any of them that was received earlier.

Nothing fires while Earshot is shutting down, and the show cannot be armed then.

Armed from the moment the words began​

A line fires a cue only if the show was Armed from the moment the words began, with no Safe in between.

  • Something said while the show is Safe never fires, even if you arm while Earshot is still working out the words.
  • Something said while Armed does not fire if Safe was pressed before the cue went out, even if you armed again in between.
  • Say it again once the show is Armed.

Here is one line, "Lighting cue forty two, go", said three ways:

When the words beganWhat happened nextResultWhat Activity says
Show was ArmedNothing. It stayed Armed until the cue went out.The cue is sent.Fired Go cue: …
Show was SafeYou armed half a second later, while the person was still talking.Dry run. Nothing is sent."Go cue" did not fire: heard before the show was armed
Show was ArmedSomeone pressed Make safe, then armed again, before Earshot finished the line.Dry run. Nothing is sent."Go cue" did not fire: Safe was pressed after this was said

The same rule holds for draft lines, for cues limited to people, and for anything waiting in a queue. Pressing Safe and then Arm never releases something from before the Safe.

Triggers that are not speech follow the same idea, tied to the moment the host received them. Activity says "the show was Safe when it was received" or "Safe was pressed after it was received".

Arm first, then speak

Arm, wait a beat, then call the cue. Do not arm in the middle of a call and expect that call to go.

What pressing Safe stops​

Already in motionWhat happens
A cue in the middle of its action list (for example during a Wait)The remaining actions do not run. Arming again later does not resume it.
OSC messages waiting for a TCP destinationUnsent data is discarded and the connection is reset, including data waiting in the operating system's send buffer (it is kept small, 8 KiB). The Activity log says what was not sent.
A MIDI messageStopped, even while its port is still opening. The message is checked again right before it goes to the driver.
A web request in progressCancelled, and any redirect is not followed.
A running shell commandStopped, together with the programs it started.

In Activity, an action cut off this way reads "not sent: the show was made Safe".

Editing one OSC destination reconnects only that destination. Pressing Safe resets all of them.

What Safe cannot recall​

Safe stops what Earshot would send next. It cannot reach into your console. Anything that has already left this computer is gone:

  • a UDP message,
  • a MIDI message already handed to the driver,
  • a web request that already arrived,
  • OSC data already delivered.
Proven on macOS only

A TCP reset is meant to throw away what is still unsent. That has been proven on macOS only, and the other end may not notice the reset at once. Stopping a shell command's child programs when you press Safe is untested on Windows. See Limits and known behaviour.

A MIDI send is on time or it does not go​

If the MIDI port is busy for more than 1 second, the send is dropped and Activity says "not sent: the MIDI port was busy for more than 1 s". A MIDI cue is never sent late and then reported as missed.

Start armed​

Under Setup, Show control, the switch Start armed makes automations able to fire as soon as Earshot starts. It is off by default. Leave it off unless the show runs unattended.

With it off, Armed is forgotten whenever the host restarts: the show comes back Safe. See what happens when the host restarts.

Two more things always leave the show Safe:

  • Importing a show file never arms. If the show was Armed when you imported, it goes Safe. See show files.
  • Arming by OSC works only over TCP, never UDP, and only at the Full control level. See OSC input.

The tray Armed tick​

Do not use the tray to arm

In the desktop app, the tray (menu bar) menu has an Armed tick box. It arms with one click, without the hold that the window requires. It has not been tried by hand in this build. Do not use it as your normal way to arm.

Small edges you should know about​

These are known gaps. None of them makes Safe unsafe, but they explain a surprise if you meet one.

  • The start of speech is noticed about 30 milliseconds late. A cue word that begins that close before you press Arm can still count as armed.
  • The other way round: right after a channel's audio starts, is paused, changes device or has a gap, a cue that begins within about a third of a second after you press Arm can be treated as "heard before the show was armed". Arm first, then speak.
  • Run now and web hooks are tied to Armed or Safe when the engine receives them, a moment (normally under a thousandth of a second) after the request arrives. A Run now pressed as a test in Safe at the very instant someone else arms could really send.
  • Pressing Safe twice. In a very rare tie between two Arm or Safe commands arriving at the same instant, a Safe can be answered with "conflict". The screen then shows the show's real state. Press Safe again.
  • A line whose beginning Earshot did not catch (it was restarting that channel) never fires. Activity says "the start of this line was missed".
  • A firing that Earshot refuses (for example because of the runaway limits) still uses up that automation's cooldown.
  • A MIDI feedback loop is limited only to about ten firings a second, until you make the show Safe. See the runaway stop.
  • Someone on the network who can forge sender addresses can get a real console's OSC trigger messages rate-limited. They cannot stop its Safe.

The full list of what has and has not been tried is in Limits and known behaviour.

Before the show​

  • The header shows Safe.
  • Every phrase automation has been tried on the Test bench, and said out loud on the real channel while Safe, then checked in Activity ("would have fired").
  • Everyone who can press Make safe knows where it is, and knows Alt+Shift+S.

The whole list is on the before-show checklist.
