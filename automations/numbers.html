How Earshot hears numbers, and what it refuses

A wrong number sends the wrong cue. So when Earshot is not sure it has the whole number, it fires nothing and writes the reason in Activity. A refusal is visible. A wrong cue is not.

This page covers what counts as speech for an automation, how a line is tidied before matching, what fires, what is refused, and how to call and write phrases so they work first time.

Only speech fires phrase automations​

  • Typed lines never fire phrase automations, whoever types them, in any channel, however they get there (the Feed, an OSC message, or another automation).
  • Automations hear what was said, not the glossary-corrected text. The glossary changes only what people read. If a cue depends on a word the recogniser mis-hears, add the mis-hearing to the automation's phrases. Each phrase trigger in the editor says so: "Automations hear what was said. Glossary corrections change only what you read."
  • Translations are never heard by automations. See Read in another language.
  • Lines from another Earshot computer never fire automations here. See linked channels.

For example, if you want "alesha go" to fire a cue but the recogniser writes "aleesha go", add aleesha go as another phrase in the automation. The glossary will fix the spelling people read. It will not help the automation.

How a line is tidied before matching​

Before matching, Earshot:

  • makes everything lower case and ignores punctuation;
  • turns spoken numbers into digits: "forty two" becomes 42, "two hundred and five" becomes 205, "four point five" becomes 4.5;
  • turns "Q42" and "Q 7" into "cue 42" and "cue 7";
  • turns "stand by" into "standby";
  • drops the filler words um, uh, er, erm, ah and hmm.

A lone "one" becomes 1 only straight after a word like cue, scene, song, page, preset, snapshot, channel, mix, track, number, step, look or deck. So "cue one" matches a phrase written cue 1, but "the one on the left" is left alone.

Your phrases are tidied the same way, so Go Cue {number} and go cue {number} are the same phrase.

Careful with "point oh"

If a line is cut off right after "point oh", it fires 12.0 even though you meant "point oh five".

What is refused, and why​

When a call is refused, Activity has a row like this:

"Lighting" did not fire: the number may be incomplete: "5 6" looks like part of a longer number, so 5 is not fired on its own
SaidWhy nothing fires
"cue 7 and 8", "cue seven and eight", "cue 100 and 5"Two numbers joined by "and" could be two cues or one. Earshot will not pick one.
"cue seven and a half"Earshot does not read "and a half" as .5. Say "cue seven point five".
"cue twenty for" (the line ends there), "cue twenty to five""for", "to", "too", "fore", "won" and "ate" sound like numbers or joins, so a number followed by one is not trusted. If the line carries on with ordinary words ("cue twenty for the reveal"), it fires.
"cue 5 6 7", "cue five six seven"A run of numbers could be one long number or three.
"cue four hundred oh five"Earshot reads "four oh five" as 405 but will not guess for "four hundred oh five". It would not be 40005, and it cannot tell what you meant. Say "four oh five".
"cue forty um two", "cue twelve point, uh, five"A filler word in the middle of a number, or with commas around it, leaves two separate numbers, and Earshot will not join them. (Earshot drops um, uh, er, erm, ah and hmm before it reads numbers, so "cue twelve point um five" still fires 12.5.)
a line that ends "cue 12 point", "cue 12 dot", "cue seven and", "cue one hundred and"The line was cut off. The rest of the number may still be coming.
"cue 12 dot com""dot" and "decimal" count as "point". A number followed by one that does not turn into a decimal number is refused.
"cue 5 hundred", "cue 5 oh", "cue zero oh five", "cue four oh five six"Not a clear single number.
"cue oh four", "cue point five"The value would be "oh" or "point", not a number.

After a refusal, say the call again, more plainly.

How to phrase a call so it fires first time​

  • Say the cue word, then the number, then stop or say a plain word: "cue forty two, go".
  • Say "point" for decimals: "cue twelve point five". Say digits after "point" one at a time or as one number: "point two five" or "point twenty five".
  • Say "four oh five" for 405, not "four hundred oh five". "One hundred and five" is fine.
  • Do not read a list of cues in one go. One cue per call.
  • Do not leave "um" or a long pause in the middle of a number.
  • Before a number, a word helps ("cue", "scene", "lights"). A phrase that starts with the number, such as {n} go, is refused more often, because "one twenty go" could be the tail of "one twenty".
Put this on the caller's desk

The short version for whoever calls the show: cue word, number, "go". One cue per call. Say "point" for point cues. No lists, no "um" inside a number.

How to write phrases​

  • Put a word between two values: fade {a} to {b}.
  • fade {a} {b} and cue {a} 5 are rejected: the automation shows a problem and never fires. Earshot cannot tell where one number stops and the next starts.
  • fade {a} and {b} and other phrases where only joining words ("and", "point", "oh", "dot", number words) sit between two values are rejected the same way: they could never fire.
  • A phrase containing a filler word (um, uh, er, erm, ah, hmm) is rejected the same way. It would match more than you expect.
  • "to" is allowed between two values because you wrote it into the phrase.

A rejected phrase puts a Problem badge on the automation, with a line saying what to change. See When an automation is not running.

Good and bad phrases at a glance​

PhraseVerdict
go cue {number}Good. A word before the number.
sound cue {n} goGood. Words on both sides.
fade {a} to {b}Good. A word between the two values.
{n} goAllowed, but refused more often when called. Put a word in front.
fade {a} {b}Rejected. Two values back to back.
cue {a} 5Rejected. A value straight before a number.
fade {a} and {b}Rejected. Only a joining word between the values.
um go cue {n}Rejected. It contains a filler word.

What Earshot cannot catch​

If the recogniser hears a clean, well-formed line that is simply wrong, Earshot cannot know.

The example in the notes is "twenty two five" heard as "twenty to five" with the phrase fade {a} to {b}: it looks like a good call and fires 20 and 5.

So:

  • Test your phrases on the Test bench. Type them the way they would be said, for example "go cue forty two".
  • Say them in rehearsal on the real comms, with the show Safe, and read Activity.
  • Keep Safe the default until you have.
Accuracy in a loud venue is unverified

Recognition has been tried only on computer-made voices and a built-in microphone. The recogniser still makes mistakes on clean recordings. Treat every cue as something to confirm. See Limits and known behaviour.

Draft text and numbers​

An automation set to Fire on: Draft text (faster, less safe) can fire before the line is finished. Earshot still waits until the number can no longer grow: after a number, a plain word must follow before a draft fires. "cue forty…" does not fire 40 while "two" may still be coming.

Draft text can also be corrected a moment later, and a draft-firing automation does not wait for that. For anything with a number in it, leave Fire on at Final text (recommended).
