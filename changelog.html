Earshot changelog

[0.5.2] - 2026-11-19​

Added​

  • Persistent local desktop diagnostic logs and Open Logs Folder in the Mac app menu and tray. Logging runs off the transcription thread, uses a bounded queue, and retains at most three 10 MiB files.
  • Voice decision logs link each utterance to signal levels, clipping, detected speech, speech-gate thresholds, model readiness, match scores/margins, naming and automation thresholds, learning progress, early exits and final name publication. Logs contain no transcript text, raw audio or voiceprint vectors.
  • Diagnostic comparisons for replies with at least 250 ms of detected speech, exposing their scores even when they are too short to name or learn.

Scope and limitations​

  • This release adds observability; it does not claim to fix recognition of very short comms replies. Naming and learning still require at least one second of detected speech per observation, and automation thresholds remain unchanged. The synthetic comms measurement found unstable embeddings for isolated single-word replies.
  • macOS Apple Silicon only. Windows and Linux updates remain on hold.

[0.5.1] - 2026-11-19​

Fixed​

  • Slow transcription or experimental overlap separation no longer switches off voice naming across every channel. Names continue through their bounded background queue while expensive turn and overlap processing pauses to let transcription catch up.
  • Repeated, consistent unknown replies can now establish a voice even when their similarity to a saved voice falls between recognition and immediate-discovery thresholds. This also combines short replies from the same channel, without mixing channels or dissimilar voices; pending observations expire after two minutes.
  • Setup now identifies CAM++ as the model required to detect and name voices, and explains that MossFormer2 separates audio without recognising people.

Validation and scope​

  • Added regressions for automatic naming under load and repeated unknown comms voices, plus a real CAM++ test with synthetic speech through simulated noisy, band-limited comms. These tests do not establish accuracy on real comms feeds.
  • macOS Apple Silicon only; Windows and Linux remain on hold. Overlap separation remains experimental, off by default and slower than real time.

[0.5.0] - 2026-11-19​

Added​

  • Automatic voice discovery on shared channels: unfamiliar voices appear as Voice 1, Voice 2 and so on without a registration or reading passage. Rename a detected voice in Setup → People; its saved voice and earlier line labels keep the same identity.
  • Separate finished transcript lines for consecutive speakers within one stretch of speech. Clear subsequent turns refine automatically learned voices; controlled voice learning remains available.
  • Optional local separation of two overlapping voices with the MossFormer2 model. Isolated tracks are split at pauses and ordered by onset, with separate replay audio and word timings for each line.
  • Apple Silicon runtime and overlap probes, with reproducible measurements in the developer documentation.

Changed​

  • macOS now bundles the Core ML-capable shared speech runtime. CPU remains the default where the installed-model benchmarks were faster.
  • Faster voice processing through reused embeddings, cached FFT and spectral tables, reused scratch buffers, a coarse turn check and four native voice-model threads on Apple Silicon.
  • macOS gives live transcription higher scheduling priority than supporting voice and translation work. Separation prepares in the background and releases its model when switched off.
  • Voice recognition starts enabled on new audio channels. Existing channels keep their previous switch setting. Learned voiceprints remain on the host and are excluded from browsers and exported shows.

Release scope and known limitations​

  • This version is released for macOS Apple Silicon only. Windows and Linux builds are on hold; their previous releases remain available.
  • Overlapping-voice separation is experimental and off by default. It recovered both reference phrases in the synthetic trial, but separating four seconds of audio took about 8.6 seconds on the development M3 Pro. It can miss or duplicate speech and is not yet suitable for low-latency live use.
  • Automatic discovery and turn boundaries have been tested with synthetic voices, not real crew or comms feeds. Very short replies, similar voices, noise and more than two simultaneous speakers remain limitations. Names are a reading aid and can be wrong.
  • The macOS app is ad-hoc signed and has a signed updater archive, but is not notarised by Apple.

[0.4.0] - 2026-11-17​

Added​

  • Offline WAV replay with JSON reports for comparing real console/comms recordings across installed speech models.
  • Expandable transcription timing in Setup → Channels separates first-draft processing time from finished-line delay and shows queued work.
  • Moonshine tiny English as an optional local draft model; finished lines can keep using the main speech model.
  • Select words or use the pencil on a finished line to correct the transcript. Admins can remember confirmed mishearings for future speech; the original remains available, and edits never fire automations.
  • Guided voice learning with a read-along passage, input meter, progress, an explicit processing state, and the option to keep learning in the background.
  • Low-latency and comms/noisy-feed starting points in each channel’s speech detection settings.
  • A cross-platform real-model fixture for isolated “go”, “yes”, “hold”, and “stop”, plus opt-in noise and competing-voice measurements.

Changed​

  • Finished lines, including adopted pause decodes, take priority over speculative work on other channels while preserving channel order.
  • Correction retries succeed when the requested text is already saved; remembered spellings cannot invalidate existing glossary aliases.
  • Read-along highlights follow repeated passages and survive hiding enrollment or leaving Setup and returning.
  • Earlier live updates, shorter minimum speech and final waits, and more leading audio. Untuned legacy defaults upgrade on startup; customized settings remain intact.
  • The neural voice detector resets during acoustically quiet pauses instead of resetting during a later word’s delayed onset.
  • Display corrections recognize production cue abbreviations such as “LXQ 912” and distinctive, unambiguous variants of known names. Numbers and automation input stay separate from these inferred corrections.
  • Transcript edits update saved history and search, discard obsolete translations, and reject stale concurrent edits.
  • Voice learning uses a focused, scrollable passage and keeps the channel’s input meter beside progress. Setup reports inputs that are starting or need attention.

Known limitations​

  • Noisy comms and overlapping-voice accuracy remain under evaluation. Isolated commands can still be missed or misheard, and results differ across platforms; detection presets and draft models do not guarantee instant or correct text.

[0.3.0] - 2026-11-16​

Added​

  • Check for Updates in the tray context menu and macOS app menu, with automatic GitHub Release checks after startup and every six hours.
  • Native update prompts with Update now, Not now and Don't remind me for this update; skipped versions remain available through manual checks.
  • Verified update downloads, download progress and a graceful transcription shutdown before installation and restart on macOS, Windows and Linux AppImage installations.
  • Signed updater artifacts and a GitHub Release manifest in the release scripts, including separate Windows EXE and MSI updates.

[0.2.0] - 2026-11-16​

Added​

  • Personal keyword alerts in Display → Personal keyword alerts. Enter your name or comma-separated words and phrases to flash this device when a new spoken or typed line mentions them. No automation or Armed mode is needed.
  • Alerts show the matching keyword, channel and transcript line for context. They clear after five seconds or when you press Dismiss. Reduced-motion settings show a steady alert.
  • A changelog in the online manual, linked from Display settings. Release notes now include the changes for their version.

Changed​

  • Updated the user guide, display instructions and screenshots for personal keyword alerts.
  • Corrected installation instructions to reflect the published macOS, Windows and Linux downloads. Installers remain unsigned; Windows and Linux installer interfaces and desktop windows have not been tested.

[0.1.0] - 2026-11-16​

Added​

  • Initial public desktop downloads: macOS Apple Silicon DMG, Windows x64 EXE and MSI, and Linux x86_64 DEB, RPM and AppImage.
  • Local speech transcription, Feed, Lanes, Focus and Archive views, typed messages, show configuration and crew access controls.
  • Phrase automations and show control through OSC, MIDI and HTTP, with Safe/Armed controls and an activity log.
  • Online manual with installation instructions, examples and screenshots.

Known limits​

  • Installers are unsigned. The headless host passed a demo startup smoke test on all three systems; Windows and Linux installer interfaces and desktop windows have not been tested.
  • No Intel Mac installer. Real comms hardware, real consoles and real venue operation remain untested. See the manual's limits before relying on Earshot at a show.
