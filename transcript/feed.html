Feed

The Feed is one merged timeline of every channel, newest at the bottom. It is the view most people leave open during a show. Each line shows the channel name in its colour, the time, and the text.

Every role can read the Feed and type into channels that allow it.

The mock show "Spring Tour at Harbour Hall". Invented lines, not a real show.

The parts of the Feed​

  • The summary line above the transcript says what you are looking at: "All channels", the names of one or two channels ("Show Caller and Stage Manager"), or a count ("4 of 8 channels").
  • The transcript in the middle. What each mark on a line means is in The screen at a glance.
  • Channels, the panel on the right. On a narrow screen it is hidden behind a Channels button.
  • The typing box along the bottom.

Show only some channels​

The Channels panel lists every channel under its group, each with a level meter, and a badge if something needs a look. A channel that belongs to no group is listed under "Other channels".

  1. Press a channel. The Feed now shows only that channel.
  2. Press another channel to add it. Press a selected channel again to take it out.
  3. Press a group's name (for example Stage) to add all its channels at once. Press it again to take them all out.
  4. Press Show all to clear the filter.

Channels you have filtered out are dimmed in the list. They are still being transcribed, and you can still see their meters.

The mock show, filtered to the Stage group: the summary reads "4 of 8 channels". Not a real show.

info

"Filtering changes this screen only. Groups are edited in Setup." Choosing channels here never changes a group, never changes what anyone else sees, and is never sent to the host. It is kept in this browser, so it is still there when you come back.

On a phone, press Channels above the transcript to open the list. While a filter is on, the button reads Filter on, so you can tell at a glance that you are not seeing everything.

The mock show on a phone-sized screen. Not a real show.

Reading back, and getting back to live​

The Feed follows the newest line. Scroll up to read back, and it stops following so the text does not move under your eyes.

  • While you are scrolled up, a Back to live button appears. It jumps to the newest line. If lines arrived meanwhile it also says how many, for example "Back to live 12 new".
  • Lines older than what is loaded are fetched as you scroll up.

Unread counts​

  • A channel in the list shows a count of lines you have not seen yet. This happens while you are scrolled up, or while the channel is filtered out.
  • When a filter hides channels that have new lines, the summary line adds, for example, "7 new on hidden channels".
  • Going back to live marks the channels you can see as read.

A console can also clear the counts with an OSC "mark read" message. That works on the host computer's own window only. See OSC addresses.

Type a line​

Typed lines are for things that need writing down but were not said on comms: "House open", "Hold: medical in row F".

  1. In the box at the bottom, pick the channel. The placeholder then reads "Type to" and the channel's name.
  2. Type the line. It can be up to 500 characters.
  3. Press Send.

The line appears in that channel with a Typed badge and the name of whoever sent it.

The mock show, filtered to Stage Manager, just after typing a line. Your own lines show "You". Not a real show.

  • A channel that does not accept typing is listed as, for example, "Video (no typing)". If you pick it, the box reads "Typing is off for this channel" and a note says "Video doesn't accept typed messages. Pick another channel." An admin sets this per channel with Allow typed messages (see Channel settings).
  • Narrowing the Feed to a single channel also points the typing box at that channel.
  • Nobody can type into a channel that comes from another Earshot computer. Those are read-only.
  • If a line cannot be sent you see "The message was not sent. Check the connection and try again."
warning

Typed lines never fire phrase automations, whoever types them and whatever they say. Typing "go cue 42" sends nothing. That is deliberate: only speech fires a phrase automation. See Triggers.

The name shown beside a typed line is the name the person gave when they joined, or the label on their personal link. It is a label, not proof of who they are.

A worked example: following the stage during a scene change​

You are the deputy stage manager on the mock show and you only care about the deck for the next ten minutes.

  1. In Channels, press Stage. The summary changes to "4 of 8 channels": Show Caller, Stage Manager, Deck Left and Deck Right.
  2. Deck Right is chatty and you do not need it. Press Deck Right. The summary now reads "3 of 8 channels".
  3. Lighting carries on talking. The summary shows "3 new on hidden channels", and Lighting shows a count of 3 in the list. Nothing is lost: it is all still being written down.
  4. Someone asks what the call was two minutes ago. Scroll up and read it. Back to live appears, and counts the new lines while you read.
  5. Press Back to live.
  6. Type a note for the record: pick Stage Manager in the box, type Scene change complete, 1 min 40, press Send. It appears with Typed and your name.
  7. When the change is done, press Show all.

None of this changed what the lighting operator sees on their own screen.

Limits​

  • The Feed shows the current session. Earlier sessions are in the Archive.
  • Filtering, text size and theme are per browser. A phone and a laptop each keep their own.
  • A screen that drops off the network catches up on the lines it missed when it reconnects. Anything said while the host was down was not written down. See When the host restarts.
