Reading the transcript

Everything Earshot hears ends up as lines of text. This page explains the parts of the screen that are the same wherever you are: the views, the header, and what each mark on a line means. The pages after it take the views one at a time.

Every role can read. Viewers, operators and admins all see the Feed, Lanes, Focus, the Archive and Activity. What differs is what each may do. See Roles.

The mock show "Spring Tour at Harbour Hall". The lines are invented. It is not a real show.

The views​

The views are along the top of the screen.

ViewWhat it is for
FeedOne merged timeline of every channel, newest at the bottom.
LanesOne column per channel or group, side by side.
FocusOne channel or group in very large type, for a second screen.
ArchivePast sessions, search, markers and export.
AutomationsBuild, test and run automations. See Safe and Armed.
ActivityThe live log of OSC, MIDI, web requests and automations. See Testing.
SetupDevices, channels, groups, models and access. Admins only.

On a phone the views are along the bottom instead: Feed, Lanes, Focus, Archive and More. More holds Automations, Activity, Setup, the API reference, and (where they apply) Install app and Sign out.

The mock show on a phone-sized screen. Not a real show, and not a real phone: Earshot has not yet been tried on real phones or tablets.

The header​

From left to right:

  • The show name. Check it is the show you expect.

  • The connection indicator. It tells you whether this screen is hearing from the host.

    It saysWhat it means
    LiveConnected. New lines arrive as they are spoken.
    ConnectingThe page has just opened and is reaching the host.
    Reconnecting"Connection lost. Reconnecting (attempt N)." The screen keeps trying by itself.
    Engine unreachable"No connection to the engine. Retrying (attempt N)." Check the network, and that the host is running and awake.
    DemoConnected, but to a demo show, not a real one. Either the host was started in demo mode ("The engine is running its demo show.") or the page is using the built-in mock ("Showing the built-in demo show, not a live engine.").

  • Safe or ARMED. Whether automations may send cues. Operators and admins get the control. Viewers see the state only. This is the most important control in Earshot: read Safe and Armed before a show.

    Safe. From the mock show.

    Armed. From the mock show: nothing was sent anywhere.

  • The display button (the "Aa" at the far right). It opens Display on this device: theme, text size, and the language you read in. See Display on this device.

When the show is Armed, a red band runs across the header and the browser tab title says "ARMED".

The mock show, armed. Not a real show.

What is on a line​

Each line is one stretch of speech (or one typed message) on one channel.

PartWhat it tells you
Channel name, in the channel's colour, with a strip of the same colour down the left edgeWhere it was said.
TimeWhen the person started speaking, in this device's local time.
The textWhat Earshot heard.
A person's name with a coloured dot, after the timeEarshot recognised the voice. See "Names on lines" below.
Typed badge, with a nameSomeone typed this line. It was not spoken.
A small pencil at the rightThe text was changed from what the recogniser heard. See below.
A small play control at the rightThe audio behind this line was saved and can be replayed. See Replay a line's audio.
A link mark beside the channel nameThe channel comes from another Earshot computer. Hovering says "From" and that computer's address. See Showing another Earshot computer's channels.

Lines Earshot writes itself (for example "Audio dropped out for 240 ms") appear in the channel they concern, set apart from speech.

Draft lines and final lines​

  • A draft line is soft grey. The person is still talking, and the words can change as more audio arrives.
  • A final line is solid. The person has stopped and Earshot has settled the text.
  • In a final line, words Earshot was unsure of are dimmed and have a dotted underline. Read those words with care.

Drafts appear only on channels where Show words as they are spoken is on (Setup, Channels), and only while Earshot is keeping up. In the first picture on this page, the grey lines at the bottom, which trail off in "…", are drafts.

Three things wait for the final line:

  • Cues set to fire on Final text (the recommended setting). See Building an automation.
  • Glossary corrections. Draft text on screen is always as heard.
  • Names on lines, the pencil and the play control.

Names on lines​

On a channel with Separate and recognise voices on this channel on, a line can carry the name of the person Earshot recognised, in that person's colour. In the first picture, the Deck Left line carries the name Priya.

  • The name arrives a moment after the line, never before it.
  • Its text alternative is "Recognised by voice. May be wrong." Treat it as a reading aid.
  • Short lines ("go", "copy") usually get no name.
  • Every role sees names.

Setting this up, and how often names are right, is in People: recognise voices.

Changed from what was heard​

A line that was changed from what the recogniser heard has a small pencil. The change can be:

  • a glossary spelling ("aleesha" became "Alesha"),
  • a number tidied up ("Q42" became "cue 42"), or
  • a show word ("stand by" became "standby").

Press the pencil ("Changed from what was heard. Show the original.") and a small note headed Heard shows the original words. Hovering the pencil shows the same thing.

The mock show in Lanes. The glossary corrected two words in this Lighting line, and the note shows what was heard. Not a real show.

  • The changed text is what is saved, searched and exported.
  • Only operators and admins see the pencil. In an Open show, and with the shared password, everyone is an operator, so everyone sees it. A personal-link viewer is never sent the original, and sees no pencil.
  • A channel with Filter profanity on never shows a pencil, because the original would show the words the filter hid.
  • The pencil and the play control sit together at the right of the line. They never change how tall a line is.
info

Automations hear what was said, not the corrected text. If a cue depends on a word that is often mis-heard, the pencil is how you find out what the recogniser actually wrote. See The glossary.

The Cloud mark​

A channel set to cloud speech carries a small cloud mark in the channel list (in the pictures on this page, FOH Audio). It reads Cloud, Cloud paused or On this computer. It is there so nobody is surprised that a channel's audio may leave the building. What each wording means is in Badges, marks and indicators, and the feature itself is in Cloud & audio.

Channel badges and "Behind"​

A channel that is listening normally shows no badge, only its level meter. A badge appears when something needs a look: Idle, Starting, Paused, Behind, No input, Offline or Error. Hover a badge for the detail. The full list is in Badges, marks and indicators.

Behind is the one you will meet most. Hovering it says "Transcription is running behind; live drafts are paused until it catches up".

  • Every line is still transcribed. Draft text stops, and final lines arrive later than usual.
  • It comes on when the backlog on that channel goes over 3 seconds, and goes off when it falls under 1 second.
  • Cues set to fire on final text fire later while a channel is Behind.
  • No names appear on lines while a channel is Behind.
  • If a gap appears in the audio, Earshot writes a line in the feed ("Audio dropped out for 240 ms") instead of hiding it.

What to do about it is in Troubleshooting.

A worked example: reading one minute of the mock show​

Open the first picture on this page again and read it from the top.

  1. The header says Spring Tour at Harbour Hall, Demo and Safe. So this is a demo show, the screen is connected, and no cue can be sent.
  2. "All channels" above the lines means nothing is filtered. The channel list on the right shows three groups: Stage (4 channels), Audio (2) and Lighting & Video (2).
  3. A Deck Left line carries Priya after its time. Deck Left has voice recognition on, and Earshot thinks it was Priya. It may be wrong.
  4. The Show Caller line "Warning on the house-out sequence, about two minutes." has a play control at its right edge. Show Caller keeps its audio, so you could replay that call.
  5. The lines at the bottom are grey and trail off. Those people are still talking.
  6. FOH Audio has a Cloud mark. That channel is set to cloud speech.

What has not been tried​

  • Earshot has been run with computer-made voices and a built-in microphone. Accuracy in a loud venue is unverified. Treat every line as something to confirm, and every cue more so.
  • The screens have not been tried on real phones or tablets.
