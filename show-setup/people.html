People and voice recognition

Use this when several people talk on one comms channel and you want to see who said what. When Earshot knows a voice, it puts that person's name beside the line. It is a reading aid. Names can be wrong.

Measured on computer-made voices only

Everything on this page about how often names appear, and how often they are wrong, comes from tests with computer-made voices. It has not been measured on real crew or on a real party line. Real people may do better or worse. Read the numbers below with that in mind.

Who can do this

Only admins can add people, learn voices and change Names on lines. Everyone else sees the list. Every role sees the names on lines.

Start without a reading passage​

  1. Install CAM++ under Models.
  2. Enable voice recognition on the channel.
  3. In Setup → People, leave Detect new voices automatically on and talk normally. Distinct voices with enough speech appear as Voice 1, Voice 2, and so on.
  4. Use a voice's pencil button to give it a name. Its stored identity stays the same, so earlier lines also show the name. Clear subsequent turns refine the profile over several observations.

Consecutive speakers can become separate finished lines without a silence between them. Drafts remain an unsplit preview. Roughly 1.5 seconds per speaker is needed for discovery and turn detection; short replies, similar voices and noise can remain unknown or be misidentified.

You can still add a person and learn their voice deliberately for a longer, controlled sample. Only admins can change People or the discovery settings.

Try overlapping voices​

Install Overlapping voices · MossFormer2 (experimental) under Models (about 245 MB), then enable Separate overlapping voices (experimental) in People. This tries to transcribe two people speaking at once locally. It prepares the model in the background; the Activity log reports when it is ready. While preparing, unavailable or busy, normal transcription continues.

Each isolated track is split at pauses using the channel's timing settings, and lines are ordered by onset. Replay audio belongs to the individual line. Names and very quiet speech can still be missed; source separation can also produce extra or incorrect words.

This is not yet a low-latency live feature. The synthetic trial on the development M3 Pro took about 8.6 seconds to separate four seconds of audio. It is off by default. More than two simultaneous speakers are not supported, and it has not been tested on real crew or comms feeds.

The mock show. Priya, Dan, Ines and Sam are invented, and no real voice has been learned.

1. Install the voice model​

Open Setup, Models, find Voice recognition (CAM++) and press Download. It is about 29 MB and you do it once. It is optional and is not marked Recommended. See Models.

Until it is installed, the People screen shows a notice that voice recognition is not available, with "Names will not appear on lines, and voices cannot be learned, until this is fixed." and an Open Models button. The Learn voice buttons say "Install the voice model first".

2. Add people​

This is optional when automatic discovery is on. To name an already detected voice, edit its existing row instead of creating another person.

  1. Open Setup, People, and press Add a person.
  2. Type a Name and pick a Colour. The name is shown beside lines in that colour.
  3. Press Save person.

Two people cannot have the same name ("Someone is already called Priya."). Each person in the list has one of three badges:

BadgeMeaning
No voice yetEarshot has never learned this person's voice. They will never be named.
Voice learnedTheir voice is stored and in use.
Voice needs learning againA voice is stored, but its record no longer fits the voice model. Learn it again.

3. Learn each voice from the channel itself​

Earshot learns a voice by listening to that person talk on the comms channel they will use.

  1. Press Learn voice next to the person (Learn again if they already have one).
  2. Under Learn from channel, pick the channel.
  3. Under How long to listen, pick 15 seconds, 20 seconds or 30 seconds.
  4. Press Start listening.
  5. Ask the person to read the displayed passage at a natural pace on that channel. The panel tells you what to say to them, for example: "Ask Sam to talk on Deck Left for about 15 seconds. Anyone else who talks will be learned too, so ask the others to stay quiet."

The mock show. Nothing is really being listened to here.

While it listens:

  • Underlined passage words have been recognized. Repeat the passage if more speech is needed; highlights survive hiding the panel or leaving Setup and returning.
  • The input meter shows whether audio is arriving. A paused or disconnected input is reported while collection waits.
  • The count is of detected speech, not of time passing. Background voices or noise mistaken for speech can affect collection. The bar reads, for example, "9 of 15 seconds of speech".
  • The panel says "Listening for Sam…", then "Hearing speech", then "More than halfway". If nothing has been heard for a couple of seconds it says "Waiting for Sam to talk…".
  • "On a noisy line, ask for longer: 20 to 30 seconds works better."
  • Only one voice can be learned at a time.
  • Earshot gives up after 3 minutes without enough speech.
  • Cancel stops learning. Hide progress keeps it running in the background; use Show progress or Review result to return.
  • Once enough speech is collected, the host explicitly switches to Processing voice. A full bar alone does not mean the voice is ready.

It ends one of four ways:

The panel saysWhat happened
"Sam's voice is learned."The voice is stored. Sam's badge becomes Voice learned.
"Sam's voice was not learned. That did not sound like one person, so nothing was changed. Learn it again with only Sam talking."More than one person was heard. The recording is not kept, and an earlier voice, if Sam had one, is left as it was.
"Sam's voice was not learned." followed by a plain messageSomething else went wrong, and the message says what.
"Cancelled. Nothing was changed."You pressed Cancel.

After a failure, press Try again.

4. Switch it on for the channel​

Open Setup, Channels, the channel, and switch on Separate and recognise voices on this channel. It is on for new audio channels; existing channels keep their previous setting. It is set channel by channel. See Channels.

5. Choose how readily names appear​

On the People screen, under the list, Names on lines has three choices. Pick a card to change it. The card in use carries an In use now badge.

ChoiceThe app saysMeasured on test voices (lines of 1 second or more from voices Earshot had learned)
Cautious"Shows a name only when Earshot is very sure. Few lines get a name; a wrong name is unlikely."About 6 in 100 got a name. Lines from people who had not been learned got no name.
Balanced (the default)"Names more lines. Sometimes a line gets the wrong name, mostly from someone whose voice was never learned, and more often if they sound like someone whose voice was."About 36 in 100 got a name. About 2 in 100 lines from people who had not been learned also got a name.
Eager"Names the most lines, and gets the most wrong."About 62 in 100 got a name. About 5 in 100 lines from people who had not been learned got a name.

Read these numbers with care. They describe controlled voice learning, not automatic discovery. They come from 21 computer-made voices (several share one speech engine), not from real people and not from a real party line. Real crew may do better or worse. In the same tests no choice ever gave one learned person's name to another learned person. But when most of the crew was learned, and the person who was not learned sounded like one who was, the unlearned person's lines were named up to 17 in 100 times at Balanced and 33 in 100 at Eager. A voice learned on clean audio and then heard through a simulated comms line got no name at all at Balanced.

The default, Balanced, was kept on the condition that this is said plainly. It may need changing after the first recording of a real party line.

Two notes sit under the cards:

  • "Automations limited to people always use the most cautious rule, whatever is chosen here." A name you can read on a line does not mean a people-limited automation will fire for it. See Automations limited to people.
  • "If two people are confused, use Learn again to give Earshot a longer, clear sample of each voice."

What a name on a line means​

The mock show. "Priya" beside the Deck Left line is what a recognised name looks like.

  • The name appears in the person's colour, a moment after the line, never before it. It never changes the line's text.
  • Its text alternative, which a screen reader reads, is "Recognised by voice. May be wrong."
  • Short lines get no name. A line needs about a second of real speech. A "go" or a "copy" usually gets none.
  • New comms voices may need several consistent replies before a label appears. Short replies are only combined within the same channel when they sound like the same person; uncertain or stale observations are discarded. Names can be missing while CAM++ is loading or its recognition queue is full. A Behind channel pauses expensive turn and overlap separation, while background voice naming continues.
  • Typed lines carry the name of whoever typed them, which is a different thing: a name someone gave themselves when they joined.
  • Every role sees names, viewers included.

Voices stay on this computer​

  • Learned voices are never sent to a browser: not to any role, not to an admin.
  • They are never in an exported show file. A show imported on another computer brings the people's names and colours and no voices.
  • Moving a show to another computer means learning every voice again. See Show files.
  • They are stored on the host, in the show file inside the data folder. See Privacy.

Forgetting a voice, deleting a person​

ButtonAsksWhat it removes
Forget voice"Forget Priya's voice?" "Earshot will stop recognising them until you learn their voice again. Lines already named keep their names."Only the voice. The person stays in the list with No voice yet.
Delete (the bin)"Delete Priya?" "Their learned voice is removed too. Automations limited to them will not fire for anyone else."The person and the voice.

The pencil edits a person's name and colour without touching their voice.

Example: four people on Deck Left​

Deck Left is a party line shared by Priya, Dan, Ines and Sam. Priya, Dan and Ines show Voice learned. Sam shows No voice yet.

  1. Sam is the gap. Until Sam is learned, Sam's lines get no name, or now and then somebody else's.
  2. Press Learn voice next to Sam. Choose Deck Left and, because the line is noisy, 30 seconds. Press Start listening.
  3. On comms: "Everyone on deck left, quiet please. Sam, talk to me for half a minute: tell me what's on the props table."
  4. Wait for "Sam's voice is learned."
  5. Check Separate and recognise voices on this channel is on for Deck Left.
  6. Watch the Feed. Longer lines from the four of them start to carry names. Short acknowledgements do not.

If Priya's lines stop being named next week, press Learn again next to Priya, on the same channel.

What to do, in practice​

  • Learn everyone who talks on the channel. Wrong names come from voices Earshot has not learned.
  • Learn them on that channel, not somewhere quiet. The same person learned on clean audio was named far less often on the noisy line.
  • On a noisy line use 20 or 30 seconds, and keep the others quiet.
  • Learn again if a voice stops being recognised.
  • Treat every name as a hint. Do not use a name to decide who gave an instruction that matters.

What has and has not been tried​

  • On a real host, with the real models and three looping recorded channels, two voices were learned through the real screen (about 36 seconds each, 16 to 17 seconds of speech). No voiceprint went to the browser.
  • At Balanced, each channel carrying one voice named every line correctly. On a channel mixing five lines from three voices, the two learned voices were named correctly (13 named lines, none wrong), and the third, never learned, got no name in the first run.
  • Not tried: real people, a real microphone, or comms-quality audio. All the voices were clean and computer-made, and several share a speech engine and so sound more alike than most people do.

More in Limits and known behaviour.

If names are not appearing​

Go down this list:

  1. Setup, Models: is Voice recognition (CAM++) installed?
  2. Setup, People: does the person show Voice learned?
  3. Setup, Channels: is Separate and recognise voices on this channel on for that channel?
  4. Is the line very short?
  5. Is the channel Behind, or did the model only just start?
  6. Was the voice learned on this channel? Is Names on lines on Cautious?
