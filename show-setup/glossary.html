The glossary

The glossary is for "words Earshot often mis-hears — names, songs, gear". You give the right spelling and the ways it tends to come out wrong, and Earshot corrects the transcript.

The one rule to remember: corrections change what you read. Automations hear what was said.

Who can do this

Only admins can open the Glossary. Everyone reads the corrected transcript.

Add a word​

  1. Open Setup, then Glossary.
  2. Press Add a word.
  3. Type the Right spelling, for example Alesha.
  4. Choose a Kind: Name, Song, Cue, Gear or Other.
  5. Under Sounds like, type how it tends to come out, for example a lesha, and press Add. Add one for each wrong spelling you have seen.
  6. Press Save glossary.

Until you save, the screen says "Not saved yet". Revert drops changes you have not saved. Search the glossary filters a long list by a word or a spelling.

The mock show. The three entries are invented, and the mock host does not check them the way a real host does.

The mock show's glossary has three entries:

With the first two saved, a line the recogniser heard as "aleesha, the lecko on the front truss is flickering" is shown, saved and searched as "Alesha, the Leko on the front truss is flickering".

The third is a useful lesson. On a real host it would be marked Not applied, because house of the rising son is made only of ordinary words. See When an entry is "Not applied" below. (The mock host in the picture does not check entries, so it shows no warning.)

How a "Sounds like" matches​

  • As whole words, ignoring capitals.
  • Split or joined spellings count as the same: with mon it or listed, "mon it or", "monit or" and "mon-it-or" all match.
  • A possessive ending is kept: "aleesha's mic" becomes "Alesha's mic". A plain plural "s" is not matched unless you list it.
  • A match never runs across a comma, a full stop or other punctuation that ends a clause.
  • A word that is already the right spelling is never changed.

The box will not take a spelling that is the same as the right spelling ("“Alesha” is the right spelling already. Type how it comes out wrong.") or one that is already listed.

To remove a word, press its bin. Earshot asks "Remove “Alesha”?" and says "It stops being corrected once you save the glossary. Lines already transcribed keep the text they have."

How corrections work​

  • Listed spellings and conservative known-name matches can be corrected. Distinctive, unambiguous near misses of known names may be corrected automatically. Add an explicit spelling for recurring misses. Names from People are also available to display correction.
  • Numbers are never changed. A correction cannot take part in a number, so text either side of "cue 12" is corrected on its own.
  • Words your automations listen for are kept. A "Sounds like" that would take one of those words away, or change it, is not applied, and its entry says why.
  • Ordinary words are not rewritten. A "Sounds like" that is an ordinary word, or made only of ordinary words, is not applied unless it is just a spelling apart from the right spelling.
  • Drafts can show corrections too. The unfinished last word is left alone until more speech arrives.
  • The corrected text is what is saved, searched, exported and translated. Removing a word later does not touch lines already transcribed.
  • Operators and admins can edit finished lines. Select the incorrect words or use the pencil, enter the correction, and save. Admins can also remember an observed mishearing for future speech. The original remains available; edits never fire automations. Concurrent conflicting edits are rejected rather than overwritten.
  • Production cue abbreviations are expanded for display. For example, LXQ 912 go becomes LX Cue 912 go; cue numbers are preserved.
  • Your words are also passed to the speech engine as hints, on engines that accept them. On a Deepgram channel that means they are sent to Deepgram. See Cloud and audio.

When an entry is "Not applied"​

Earshot refuses a correction that could rewrite ordinary speech, touch a number, or change a word an automation listens for. The entry then shows Not applied: and the reason, and the top of the screen says, for example, "2 entries are not being applied. Each one says why below. The rest still work."

What you enteredWhy it is not appliedWhat to do
Sounds like light for "Lightwalker""'light' is an ordinary word; this alias would rewrite normal speech". Every "turn the light on" would become "turn the Lightwalker on".Use a spelling that is not an ordinary word on its own, such as lite walker.
Sounds like house of the rising son, or the light for "Theo"It "is made of ordinary words, including a small word like 'of'"."Add a distinctive word to it", or accept that this one cannot be corrected safely.
Sounds like down stage for "Downstage Dan"Every word is an ordinary word and it is not a mis-spelling of the right spelling. (clear calm for "Clear-Com" is allowed, because it is only a spelling apart.)Use a sounds-like that is a mis-spelling of the word.
Right spelling Cue 12, Twelve or First ElectricThe word contains something that is read as a number ("'first', which is read as a number"). Numbers are never changed.Rewrite it without that word.
Sounds like aleesha 2The spelling contains a number.Remove the number.
Sounds like you go for "Hugo", while an automation listens for go"'you go' would remove 'go' from the text", and an automation listens for that word. A refusal on one spelling does not stop the entry's other spellings, or any other entry.

Because the protected words come from your automations, an entry can become "Not applied" when you add or change an automation, and come back when you remove it. Look at this screen after editing automations.

Automations hear what was said, not the corrected text​

The glossary changes only what people read. An automation is matched against what the recogniser heard, with numbers and show words tidied, and without glossary corrections. Each phrase trigger in the automation editor says so: "Automations hear what was said. Glossary corrections change only what you read."

So if a cue depends on a word the recogniser gets wrong, add the mis-hearing to the automation's phrases.

Example. You want "alesha go" to fire a cue. The recogniser writes "aleesha go".

  • In the glossary, Alesha with Sounds like aleesha makes the Feed read "Alesha go". Good for readers. It does nothing for the cue.
  • In the automation, set Match mode to Any of and give it two phrases: alesha go and aleesha go. Now the cue fires whichever way it is heard.

See Triggers for match modes, and try the phrase on the Test bench.

If another admin saved while you were editing​

Earshot does not overwrite their work. It says "The glossary was changed on another device." and offers two buttons:

ButtonWhat happens
Load the new versionTheir glossary replaces what is on your screen. Entries you had not saved are listed under "Your unsaved entries, not in the new version:", each with Add back.
Replace it with mineYour version is saved over theirs.

What the glossary cannot do​

  • It cannot fix a word the recogniser hears a different way every time. Only exact listed spellings are replaced.
  • It cannot change a number, on purpose. A wrong number sends the wrong cue. See How Earshot hears numbers.
  • It does not change draft text. A draft line on screen is as heard. The correction lands when the line is final.
  • It will not replace an ordinary word or phrase, even if the recogniser keeps writing one in place of a name.
  • On a channel set to cloud speech, your glossary words leave the building with the audio.

Moving to another computer​

The glossary is part of the show file. Export show file includes it, and an import replaces this computer's glossary with the one in the file. See Show files.
